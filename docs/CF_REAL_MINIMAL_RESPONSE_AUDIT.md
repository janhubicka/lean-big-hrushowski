# Adversarial audit: a minimal C_F response with published real-growth assumptions

17 October 2026

## Mathematical result

Let F be the real growth function of the sparse-graphs paper:
F(0)=0, monotone nondecreasing and unbounded above. Its
restriction Fnat: ℕ→ℝ has the same properties on integer
cardinalities. The previously certified exact generalized
inverse defines K(k) to be the greatest integer N for which
Fnat(N)≤2(k+1). The numerical theorem verifies the inverse
domination property required by the tagged construction.

Suppose H is a finite growth-class graph on S⊕B, with a
minimal *strict* d-closed inclusion of its complete old part
S, and f:S→U_n is a prescribed induced embedding with
strictly d-closed image in the actual finite tagged stage.
The already certified arbitrary-minimal-response theorem
constructs a complete induced copy of H over f[S], using
the actual named support and ordered fresh block, and
makes its image globally strictly d-closed in the
countable birth graph.

The only difference from the numerical conditional version
is that K and its domination proof are now constructed from
the actual real growth function, not supplied independently.
The old source is fixed pointwise under birth labels.

## Five hostile mathematical checks

1. **Inverse correct even on plateaus.** The generalized
integer inverse is characterized by the exact biconditional
Fnat(N)≤2(k+1) iff N≤K(k). No strict monotonicity,
continuity or unique inverse value is assumed.

2. **Real to integer unboundedness.** Unboundedness of the
real function does not imply unbounded values on an arbitrary
subsequence without a monotonicity assumption. Here
monotonicity and Archimedean integer cofinality verify
unboundedness of the cardinality restriction.

3. **Class membership and strict minimality.** The
entire finite target graph H must belong to C_F and
its full old-to-new step must be minimal strictly
d-closed. The inverse alone does not supply either
graph-theoretic property.

4. **Full induced extension.** The resulting map preserves
and reflects all old-old, old-fresh and fresh-fresh
adjacencies, including missing boundary edges.
The old embedding remains the given f, not an unrelated
abstract isomorphic copy.

5. **Scope of genericity.** This one-step result is
conditional on the old source already being given as
an induced strictly closed subset of a finite stage,
and on the requested target being a complete split
graph S⊕B. The source-lift and finite-step
representation theorems supply the separate bridges.
The complete extension-property induction has not
yet been certified by this theorem.
